# Paying Off High-Interest Debt

## Avalanche vs. Snowball
- **Avalanche**: pay minimums on everything, throw extra cash at the
  highest-interest debt first. Mathematically optimal — minimizes total
  interest paid.
- **Snowball**: pay minimums on everything, throw extra cash at the
  smallest-balance debt first. Behaviorally optimal for some people — quick
  wins build momentum and adherence.

## When avalanche clearly wins
When the interest rate gap between debts is large (e.g. a 24% APR credit card
vs. a 6% APR student loan), the interest savings from avalanche become large
enough that snowball's momentum benefit rarely makes up the difference.

## A common trap
Making only minimum payments on a credit card at 20%+ APR while simultaneously
building savings in an account earning under 5%. The guaranteed "return" of
paying down 20%+ APR debt almost always beats the savings account yield,
except for the first small emergency buffer, which should exist even while
paying down high-interest debt.
